The promise behind “once”

A person’s chart.
A lasting entitlement.

  1. Identify the birth detailsDate, time and resolved birthplace. Changing a name, language or chart style costs nothing.
  2. Save the result and ownership togetherOne successful acquisition, one charge, even if two devices request it together.
  3. Restore without another purchaseReinstall, cache eviction or a backend upgrade never creates a new fee for that same chart.

The promise behind every charge

Reserve first.
Settle when it works.

Quote→Reserve→Complete→Charge

A temporary hold protects your balance while work happens. The final debit is tied to a complete, saved result.

  • Retries and double taps reuse the same operation.
  • Known failure releases the hold.
  • Unknown outcomes reconcile before another attempt.
  • A refund is recorded against its original transaction.

A corrected birth date, time or place may need a new chart. Show the difference and its price before proceeding. Simply returning to an already owned revision remains free.

05 / The system underneath

Designed to be trusted.

Read the complete architecture ↗
Can one credit work for AI, reports, remedies and calls?

Yes as a common pricing unit, with clear payment eligibility. Start with Digital credits for charts, AI and reports. Keep a separate eligible Services balance or checkout for qualifying live calls and physical remedies. A large digital balance should never misleadingly appear spendable on everything.

Apple’s digital-purchase rules, its non-expiring purchased credits requirement, and its one-to-one/physical-goods provisions affect this design. Google also distinguishes digital currency from physical goods and qualifying one-to-one services without replay. Apple guidelines · Google payment guidance.

Third-party astrologer settlements need a provider and legal review. RBI’s closed-system definition concerns the issuer’s own goods/services and excludes third-party settlement. Calling it a credit does not decide its classification. RBI PPI directions, section 2.1.

How do we prevent duplicate charges and missing credits?

Keep an immutable ledger, funding lots, atomic holds and account-scoped operations. Verify store payments on the server and credit each transaction once. Bind each quote to its inputs, account and price version. Commit the charge, saved result and entitlement together. Reconcile store, ledger and provider records every day.

Different request IDs for the same unowned Kundali still share one acquisition. An expired or released hold cannot be charged by a late worker. Purchased credits never expire; refunds follow the original payment route and lot allocation.

What happens to subscriptions, old wallets and saved charts?

Honor active subscriptions through their existing paid period and avoid charging credits for included benefits. Stop offering new subscriptions only when the credit rollout is ready. Backfill saved charts as owned at no charge. Preserve existing wallet value and eligibility with an audited migration. No automatic subscription cancellation is part of this plan.

What will the admin panel manage?

Versioned prices, store package mappings, welcome-grant budgets, pending holds, verified payments, refund reasons, cost and margin, abuse limits, and reconciliation exceptions. Every adjustment leaves an audit record. Support can explain any balance from its transactions.

How are welcome credits and all AI entry points controlled?

Grant 50 credits once per eligible verified account, not once per install. AI chat, group chat, assistant panels, palm/face, reports, voice and transcription all pass server authorization. Included voice gets a bounded allowance attached to the original action; no hidden auto-read fee. Local calculations and owned-content reads remain free.

What remains to decide before implementation?

Actual supplier costs, target margin, storefront price points, tax treatment, existing free promises, included voice allowance, subscription migration, and the merchant model for calls/remedies. This page is a product and architecture proposal; it does not change the current app’s billing.
